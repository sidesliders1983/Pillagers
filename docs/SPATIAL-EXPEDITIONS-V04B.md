# Spatial Expedition Groups v0.4B — #148

This opt-in `/fjord-play?discovery=1` prototype extends the existing reusable Core groups with typed physical Explore Area orders. It does not enable exploration by default or replace historical four-region Recon, Surveillance and Pillage missions. Ordinary residents still receive no individual movement orders. Physical rival presentation remains #149; spatial raids remain #150.

## Player flow

With nothing selected, choose **Create exploration group** in the contextual management panel. Core creates an empty reusable **Group 1**, **Group 2**, etc.; no name or member form is required. Select a resident on the island, choose a group and use **Add to group**. A resident already assigned to a group can **Leave group** while home. Clear the selection to see each group as its own expandable section with its membership, status, **Explore Area** and **Return Home** actions. Menu → Expedition groups is a shortcut to this no-selection view. Empty groups cannot depart; active groups cannot change membership. Choose **Explore Area**, tap a land destination, review the planned round-trip Food and tick estimate and select **Send group**. If a tap lands on an unavailable navigation cell, Core may propose walkable ground within 8 m on the same connected local surface. The pale objective ring moves to that actual destination and the proposal explains the adjustment before confirmation. It never snaps across water or a steep ridge. If no safe nearby point exists, the user can tap another destination or cancel; no Food is spent. Cancel target has no cost; **Return Home** after departure recalls every member along their actual travelled route.

Residents retain existing work-age, caregiver, availability and exclusive-membership rules. Away members stop home labour and use the existing absence rules for annual consumption, families, careers and care. They remain the same named/DNA-shaped entities in 3D. Their daily Living World movement controller does not move them while on an expedition.

## Owner-approved experimental values

- Speed: 2 metres per canonical tick.
- At the target: 25 ticks, followed by automatic return.
- Food: 1 per member for each started 125 planned ticks, including both travel legs and the target stay, paid at departure.
- A rejected/no-route order mutates nothing. Recall refunds no Food, and does not erase confirmed discoveries.
- Explore Area introduces no extra random casualties or weather speed/risk coefficients. Existing natural mortality still applies; all members dying ends the mission as Lost. Previously purchased provisions cover the trip even if home stocks later reach zero. Historic Recon/Surveillance/Pillage rules are unchanged.

Policy is saved in the optional version-1 `world.spatial` extension. A 200 m outward route at 2 m/tick takes 100 + 25 + 100 = 225 ticks: two provisions per member. For individual routes, the longest outward journey determines the common provision estimate: early arrivals wait at the target, and the 25-tick stay begins when everyone has arrived. All members carry provisions for that complete group schedule. The Core, not animation or camera position, owns progress and all outcomes.

## Terrain, visibility and presentation

Planning uses bounded deterministic A* over the existing final walkability grid, cardinal steps, native canonical height queries, dry ground and a maximum 0.65 local grade. Search expands at most 40,000 nodes; an exhausted/unreachable request fails clearly and asks for a nearer goal. It does not promise a route across water or implement new boat missions. Existing household/reserved foundation footprints and the middle place are excluded from new routes. Each resident starts at their exact current rendered ground position, captured in the dispatch command and saved as the first route point. A sampled safe connector joins that position to the existing navigation grid. A resident already within a parcel may exit that containing parcel; the connector cannot cross another reserved footprint. No new terrain classification, terrain flattening or saved second heightfield is introduced.

Dispatch stores the validated route but reveals none of it. Confirmed progress refreshes the existing clan-specific observation mask at 8 m distance intervals and phase changes. Every refresh combines settlement/landing observers with the actual confirmed positions of living expedition members, deduplicating coincident observers. At most 16 parties and 62 residents can explore simultaneously, keeping the existing 64-observer discovery budget including the two founding observers. Departed terrain remains mapped, while current visibility follows actual observers. The offer exposes an explicitly qualified planned-round-trip estimate; it exposes no route geometry, hidden terrain classification, rival names, residents or inventory.

The render layer interpolates only between confirmed route distances, follows that same route around corners and samples canonical ground height. Each real member follows their own route with the native Run clip; the run animation does not increase the configured canonical speed. Early arrivals idle at the destination. Recall reverses each member immediately from their own reached point, and shorter returns wait for the rest of the party. It never predicts future Core ticks. Previously saved prototype missions without individual routes retain their shared-route semantics. Pause, camera changes, frame rate, culling and hidden tabs cannot advance a mission or reveal terrain. Saving during a visual fade preserves committed knowledge.

## Save/load and headless replay

Core JSON saves individual routes (including dispatch-time origins), target, membership, provision charge, policy, phase, distance, return-start progress and timestamps. No renderer is needed to continue an already-dispatched journey. The authoritative blueprint is reattached by `FjordCampaign` after the existing versioned geography is regenerated. A bound geography context is mandatory to advance an active physical mission. Core-only tooling must provide the same context explicitly; it must not silently advance against unrelated geography. Import checks identity, route topology and canonical walkability before committing. Historical saves without `world.spatial` retain their previous semantics and gain no invented missions.

Departure and return have factual events and participant references. Food provisions appear as spending in the existing Clan Book factual ledger. Mission reports list current phase/outcome and the original charge; no looted resources or fictitious rival encounters are generated by Explore Area.
